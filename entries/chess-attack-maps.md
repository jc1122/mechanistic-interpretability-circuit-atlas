# Chess attack maps: a hint scratchpad makes attack maps learnable; answer-only does not
**Task.** Given a board and a colour c, output the 64-bit map of squares attacked by c (friendly-occupied squares count; labels checked against python-chess). C1: one piece, all 768 (piece, square) cases, 384/384 split per seed; C2: 1-9 sliders of random colour; C3: both kings + random armies. C2/C3 train on fresh boards every batch and test on 2048 fixed fresh boards (exact = every scored token right). Formats: A = map only; S = map then square list; T = list only; H = hint scratchpad (per colour-c piece: its square, then per direction a marker and the attacked squares nearest-first), then the map.
**Status.** 2026-11-09. Screening (A/S/T, PART and TRAIN, 3-10 seeds per cell), retries (H format, width/depth, data, budget), final run: C3 H TRAIN and C2 H TRAIN, 10 seeds each, wd .1, 60k steps. Solved (test exact 1.0): C3 7/10 (10/10 >= .998), C2 1/10 (3/10 >= .99). Closed form: none accepted.
## Summary
Answer-only nets never form the "nearest piece on the ray" read (0 solved in every A/S/T cell; one 6-layer C3 A seed of 5 at .997). With the hint scratchpad a from-scratch 4-layer net solves C3 (7/10 at 1.0, 10/10 >= .998) and C2 at d512 (1/10 at 1.0, 3/10 >= .99). Every net that reaches >= .93 uses one circuit type, T1 "count block + late contextual pointers + MLP ray step" (C3 10/10, C2 4/4); C2 failures (6/10) lack only the asked colour at the first scratch token. No closed form survives substitution plus resample ablation.

[METHODS.md](../METHODS.md). Decoder, ReLU, NoPE, no LayerNorm. AdamW lr 1e-3, wd .1, betas (.9, .98), warmup 1000, cosine over 60k, clip 1, batch 64 (micro 16), eval every 250 steps, plateau stop 5000 after 45k; PART fp32, TRAIN TF32; one GPU per pack of 2-5 seeds. Screening: A/S/T PART 8k, TRAIN <= 25k; H TRAIN 20k, then 60k. Final: C3 H seeds 10-19 (d256), C2 H seeds 10-19 (d512). Diagnostics on 32 boards, substitution on 128. ## Hand-built reference
A: L0 builds square/colour coordinates; each L1 ray head attends to the nearest occupied square on its ray (score Rel(q,k) + G*occupied(k), Rel = G - A*distance, BOS 1.5G); leaper/pawn heads read fixed offsets; a colour-gated OR neuron per square: y = step(16(AW + M*CW - M)) + step(16(AB + M*CB - M)), step(z) = ReLU(z) - ReLU(z-1). H: L1 reads the current piece/direction and target occupancy, L2 one-step heads move along the ray (nx = cur + g unless cur occupied), the map head tests "square s appears in the scratch". Exact: C1 all 768; A C2 5000 random + 5000 OOD, C3 5000 + 4096 playouts; H C2/C3 3000 each. Used size A C3 14 heads + 264 neurons; H 24 + 1041 (task part 22 + 397). One HAND only (no alternative HAND).

## Closed form
None accepted (bar: substitution exact and resample exact of the claimed circuit each >= .90 x net exact). Claim: L0 heads = uniform causal mean, L3 pointer heads = one-hot on their dominant key, rest trained. Substitution: L0 uniform 0/13 nets; L3 pointers C3 .30 .97 .41 1.0 .68 0 .88 .34 .07 1.0 (s10-s19), C2 s13 .93, s14 .16. Complement keeping L0, L3 and MLPs (L1-L2 heads resampled), C3 s12, s15 and C2 s14: resample exact 0 (zero 0, mean 0). Discovered circuits (14 nets): held-out .375-.92; complement resample .55-.89, all below .90 (circuit resampled 0, random same-size 0). Feature level: at the 4 sites where a sparse dictionary splice is exact, no feature tracks a HAND variable (c corr <= .22, y <= .35, near the random-basis baseline), so no feature-level closed form can be stated (cf. Korznikov et al. 2026).
## Weights excerpt and reading
HAND constants: ray score G - A*distance + G*occupied, BOS 1.5G; OR neuron gain 16, offsets M*(CW - 1); H one-step head = Rel(next(cur), k). Trained (no certified closed form): per L0 head sv2/sv1 of W_Q^T W_K and per L3 head the |W_O| block norm, from the fused QKV slices of the archived nets (printed by the archive's excerpt script); 83/136 L0 QK maps of 17 C3 final snapshots are near rank-1 (sv2/sv1 <= .05), consistent with near-uniform counting heads whose OV writes token counts; the pointer content arrives at L3.
## Comparison
Same variables as HAND-H (cur, g, y decodable at R2 >= .93; y causally used), different layout: HAND does the ray step in L2 with one head per step and the map with one head; the trained nets add an L0 count block, need L1-L2 heads, point at L3 and step in MLPs, one to two layers later. Scratchpad vs answer-only (C3): 10/10 vs 0/10 at 4 layers. Board-state probing in language models (Toshniwal et al. 2022) finds state tracked but full legal-move sets weak, as in the A cells. Sparse attention emerges after a plateau that shortens with pattern repetition (Zucchet et al. 2025): C2's sparse random-colour boards explain its 3/10. Middle-layer heads vary most across seeds (Stanley et al. 2026), matching the per-seed pointer sets here.
## Controls and limits
Legacy final: wd .1 only (no wd sweep), host and device not logged. Diagnostics n = 32 boards; interchange nf < 30 (cur, g) gives no verdict, c untested (nf 0). HAND-free discovery read on C3 10/10 and C2 10/10 seeds. Neuron counts and HAND effective rank not measured. Archived accuracy (`acc_test_fp32` in the manifest, fp32 on the shipped `test_C2`/`test_C3` files) is on a different 2048-board draw than `recorded_test` (the training run's test boards, drawn on the training GPU and not reproducible on other devices); weights are bit-identical and three GPU forward paths agree, so the gap is the board draw, not precision. Beyond the archive tolerance (1 board best, 3 final; the other 11 TRAIN nets within 2 boards): C3 best s14 .999 vs 1.0, C2 best s13 .9951 vs .9917, C2 final s13 .9932 vs .9902; two equally valid draws move a net by up to 14 of 2048 boards.
